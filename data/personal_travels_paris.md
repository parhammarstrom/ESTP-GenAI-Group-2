# Personal Travel Report: A Tourist Visit to Paris

## Overview

This report documents a personal travel experience visiting Paris, France, over a period of 8 days. The trip was planned as a leisure journey combining cultural sightseeing, gastronomic exploration, and leisurely strolls through one of the world's most iconic cities.

## Trip Details

- **Destination:** Paris, France
- **Duration:** 8 days, 7 nights
- **Travel Style:** Independent tourism, self-guided
- **Budget Category:** Mid-range
- **Season:** Late September (shoulder season)
- **Group Size:** 2 travelers (couple)

## Pre-Trip Planning

### Booking and Reservations

Flights were booked 3 months in advance through an online travel agency. A direct flight from Amsterdam to Paris Charles de Gaulle Airport was selected, with a duration of approximately 1 hour and 15 minutes.

Hotel accommodation was booked through a booking platform. The choice was a 4-star boutique hotel in the 6th arrondissement, near Saint-Germain-des-Prés. The room included:

- King-size bed
- Free Wi-Fi
- Daily breakfast (continental)
- Air conditioning
- City view

The total accommodation cost was approximately €210 per night, including taxes.

### Documents and Insurance

- Valid passport (EU citizen, so no visa required)
- European Health Insurance Card
- Private travel insurance covering cancellation, medical emergencies, and lost luggage
- Printed copies of all reservations kept in a separate folder

### Itinerary Planning

A loose day-by-day itinerary was prepared, focusing on one major area or theme per day to avoid exhaustion. Key principles:

- Limit to 2-3 major attractions per day
- Walk between nearby sights rather than using transport
- Leave afternoons flexible for spontaneous discoveries
- Reserve evening slots for dinner in local restaurants

## Day-by-Day Report

### Day 1: Arrival and First Impressions

Arrived at Charles de Gaulle Airport at 9:40 AM. The RER B train was used to reach the city center, taking approximately 35 minutes to Saint-Michel station. A shared taxi from the station to the hotel cost an additional €8.

Hotel check-in was at 2 PM. The room was comfortable, clean, and well-located. A light walk around the neighborhood followed, including a stop at a local bakery for fresh croissants and a visit to the small square in front of the hotel.

The first evening was relaxed. Dinner was at a bistro on Rue de Buci, where we ordered:

- Soupe à l'oignon (onion soup) — €9.50
- Steak-frites — €22
- A glass of Bordeaux red wine — €8
- Café and crème brûlée — €7

Total: approximately €47 for two.

### Day 2: The Louvre and Tuileries Garden

The Louvre Museum was visited first thing in the morning to avoid the largest crowds. A timed-entry ticket (€22) was purchased online in advance. The visit lasted approximately 4 hours, with focus on:

- The Denon wing (Mona Lisa, Winged Victory of Samothrace)
- The Sully wing (Egyptian antiquities)
- The Richelieu wing (European paintings)

A bench lunch was taken in the Tuileries Garden, which stretches from the Louvre to Place de la Concorde. The garden is free to enter and offers a peaceful green space with manicured lawns, fountains, and outdoor cafés.

In the afternoon, a walk along the Seine River to the Pont Alexandre III was completed. The bridge is considered one of the most ornate in the world, with gilded sculptures and decorative lampposts.

### Day 3: Montmartre and Sacré-Cœur

The entire day was spent in Montmartre, the historic artist's quarter in the 18th arrondissement.

**Morning:**
- Took the funicular to the top of the hill (€1.70 each way)
- Visited Sacré-Cœur Basilica (free entry to the church; €8 for the dome with panoramic views)
- Explored Place du Tertre, where street artists paint portraits and caricatures

**Afternoon:**
- Wandered the cobblestone streets of Montmartre
- Visited the Maison de van Gogh, a small museum dedicated to Vincent van Gogh's years in Montmartre (entry: €10)
- Stopped at a local cheese shop to sample various regional cheeses

**Evening:**
- A concert was attended at the Moulin Rouge (cabaret show, tickets: €85 per person)
- Dinner afterward at a small restaurant in the neighborhood (€35 per person for a 3-course meal)

### Day 4: Versailles Day Trip

A full day trip to the Palace of Versailles was undertaken.

**Transport:**
- RER C train from Saint-Michel to Versailles Rive Gauche (35 minutes, €4.35 per person)
- Return train in the evening

**Visit:**
- Palace entry with the grounds: €21 per person
- The Hall of Mirrors, state apartments, and the King's bedroom were the highlights
- The formal gardens and fountains were explored on foot (the park is vast; about 3 hours was spent here)
- The Marie-Antoinette Estate (Hamlet) required a separate ticket: €11

**Lunch:**
- A picnic was set up in the gardens with items from a local supermarket (bread, cheese, charcuterie, fruit, and water). Total cost: approximately €15 for two.

**Evening:**
- Returned to Paris around 8 PM
- A simple dinner at a pizzeria near the hotel (€20 per person)

### Day 5: Musée d'Orsay and the Left Bank

**Morning:**
- The Musée d'Orsay was visited (entry: €16). The collection of Impressionist and Post-Impressionist masterpieces was the main draw:
  - Monet's water lilies and haystacks
  - Renoir's impression of the Ballet
  - Degas's dancers
  - Van Gogh's self-portraits
  - Cézanne's still lifes

**Afternoon:**
- Strolled through the Left Bank (Rive Gauche)
- Visited the Shakespeare and Company bookstore on Rue de la Bûcherie, a famous English-language bookshop
- Browsed the antique bookshops along the Seine
- Sat at a terrace café on Boulevard Saint-Germain (two espressos and a slice of tarte au citron: €12 total)

**Evening:**
- Dinner at a small brasserie in the 5th arrondissement
- Menu: coq au vin (€24), ratatouille (€14), a bottle of Sancerre white wine (€28), and a tarte Tatin for dessert (€8)
- Total: approximately €74 for two

### Day 6: The Seine, Latin Quarter, and Panthéon

**Morning:**
- A Seine River cruise was taken (Bateaux Parisiens, 1-hour tour, €14 per person). The boat passed under all the major bridges and past landmarks including:
  - Notre-Dame Cathedral (exterior, as the interior was still under restoration)
  - The Eiffel Tower
  - The Musée d'Orsay
  - The Conciergerie

**Afternoon:**
- Explored the Latin Quarter (5th arrondissement)
- Visited the Panthéon (entry: €13), which houses the remains of notable French figures including Voltaire, Rousseau, and Curie
- Climbed to the dome for a view over the city
- Walked through the Luxembourg Garden (free entry), where a bench rest and people-watching session took place

**Evening:**
- A farewell dinner at a traditional French restaurant in the Marais district
- Tasting menu for two (5 courses): €95
- A glass of Champagne: €15
- Total: approximately €110

### Day 7: Le Marais, Shopping, and Local Markets

**Morning:**
- Explored the Le Marais district (3rd and 4th arrondissements)
- Visited Place des Vosges, the oldest planned square in Paris, with its red-brick buildings and symmetrical gardens
- Browsed the vintage clothing shops and artisan stores in the area
- Picked up a few souvenirs: a silk scarf, a small box of Macarons from Ladurée (€14 for 6), and a book on French history

**Afternoon:**
- Visited the Marché des Enfants Rouges, one of the oldest covered markets in Paris
- Sampled street food: crêpes, fresh oysters, and a cheese platter
- Took a cooking class at a local kitchen studio (2.5 hours, €65 per person), where we learned to make:
  - French omelette
  - Quiche Lorraine
  - A simple pot-au-feu

**Evening:**
- A casual dinner at a crêperie (galettes with various fillings: €18 per person)

### Day 8: Departure Day

- Checked out of the hotel at 11 AM
- A final walk along the Seine to the Eiffel Tower for one last look
- Took the RER B back to Charles de Gaulle Airport
- Flight departure at 5:15 PM
- The trip concluded smoothly with all luggage accounted for and no issues

## Transportation Notes

- **Paris Metro:** A 10-ride Navigo Easy card (€17.35) was the most convenient option. Each single ticket costs €2.15.
- **RER (suburban rail):** Used for airport transfers and the Versailles trip.
- **Walking:** The majority of daily movement was on foot. Paris is very walkable in the central arrondissements.
- **Bicycle:** A Vélib' (public bike) 1-day pass (€5) was used on Day 5 for a ride along the Canal Saint-Martin.
- **Taxis:** Used only once (airport to hotel) for approximately €55.

## Dining Summary

The food experience was a highlight of the trip. Notable meals and observations:

- **Bakeries (boulangeries):** The fresh baguette and croissant culture is unmatched. Most mornings started with a purchase from the neighborhood bakery.
- **Bistros:** Small, family-run restaurants offering traditional French cuisine at reasonable prices. The portions are generous and the wine list is often excellent.
- **Cafés:** Parisians have a café culture. Sitting at a terrace, ordering an espresso (€1.50) or a café crème (€2.50), and watching the city go by is a tradition worth experiencing.
- **Markets:** Local food markets (Marché d'Aligre, Marché des Enfants Rouges) offer fresh produce, cheeses, and street food at fair prices.
- **Fine dining:** One evening was spent at a moderately upscale restaurant in the 8th arrondissement. The tasting menu was €85 per person for 6 courses, with wine pairing at an additional €45.

## Cultural Observations

- Parisians are known for their fashion sense, and the city offers a constant display of well-curated personal style.
- The pace of life in the central arrondissements is slower than in the business districts. Many shops close for a long lunch break (12:30 PM – 2:30 PM).
- Tipping is not mandatory but rounding up or leaving 5% is appreciated for good service.
- The French language is widely spoken. While English is common in tourist areas, attempting basic French phrases (bonjour, merci, s'il vous plaît) was well-received.
- The city is built for strolling. The boulevards, gardens, and riverside paths invite long, unhurried walks.

## Practical Tips from This Trip

1. **Book major museum tickets online in advance** to skip long lines, especially at the Louvre and Versailles.
2. **Walk as much as possible.** The best discoveries often happen in unplanned side streets.
3. **Eat where the locals eat.** Restaurants within 3-4 blocks of major tourist sites tend to be better and cheaper.
4. **Carry a small umbrella** even in September. Rain can appear suddenly and without warning.
5. **Get a local SIM card or ensure roaming is set up** before departure. The metro system is extensive but a phone map is helpful for planning routes.
6. **Carry a small daypack** for water, snacks, and a light jacket. Layers are useful as indoor spaces are often warm while outdoors it can be cool.
7. **Respect shop hours.** Many smaller shops close on Tuesdays or Wednesdays, and all shops close at 7 or 8 PM.
8. **The Navigo Easy card** is the most practical for 2-3 days of metro travel. For a full week, a weekly Navigo pass (€26.50) may be more economical.

## Final Thoughts

Paris delivered an exceptional experience that balanced iconic landmarks with intimate, local moments. The city's density of museums, parks, historic architecture, and culinary offerings makes it nearly impossible to see everything in a single visit. The trip left a strong desire to return, particularly in the spring when the gardens are in full bloom and the days are longer.

The overall experience was rated as a 9/10. The only minor drawbacks were the long lines at the Eiffel Tower (which was visited from the outside to avoid the 2-hour wait) and the occasional difficulty finding English menus in smaller restaurants.
